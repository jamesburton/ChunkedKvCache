## Verdict

Gap: "per-layer deviation profiling incl. MoE routing drift, and freeze-early/recompute-deep tiering".

- Per-layer KV-deviation profiling (dense models): PARTLY COVERED. 2603.20218 (VERIFIED in HTML) shows per-layer dKV selection differs by layer, early-layer picks collapse onto chunk-start tokens, mid/late layers spread beyond chunk starts, and the optimal token set is layer-dynamic. CacheClip and Kamera (abstract only) assert that the cross-chunk residual matters most / concentrates in deep layers. What I did not find: a systematic per-layer x token-type (and static graph role, e.g. definition sites) deviation heatmap, or a "% of deviation captured by top-k tokens per layer" curve. Kamera's deep-layer/low-rank claim is multimodal and unconfirmed beyond the abstract.
- Layer-tiered recompute (freeze early, recompute deep on k%): PARTLY COVERED, no exact match found. Closest: RelaxKV (layer-specific repair targets, query-guided), CacheClip (deep-layer selection signal), and a Droidspeak-style layer band (second-hand, cross-model, UNVERIFIED). CacheBlend already prunes the recomputed set by depth (r1 > r2). No paper found that tests "early layers reused as-is, deep layers recompute k%" against uniform selection at equal FLOPs. Note the structural caveat from INCEPTION 1: recomputing a token at a deep layer requires its earlier-layer states (RelaxKV snippet says exactly this), so true savings come from reducing the set of tokens per layer, not skipping early layers wholesale; this should be framed accordingly.
- MoE routing drift under isolated vs full-context encoding: OPEN as far as found. No paper measures expert-assignment agreement between independently encoded chunks and full-context prefill. Adjacent evidence only: MoEZip (routing flips under KV eviction, abstract-level) and 2604.17182 (routing is context-dependent in mid layers L14-20 for decode branches). Both suggest drift is plausible, which motivates E6, but neither answers it. Confidence limited by search coverage (only a few queries; negative result).
- Hidden-state caching: HCache (EuroSys '25) covers hidden-state restoration for exact-prefix state, not cross-chunk blending (UNVERIFIED details). Combination of hidden-state chunk caching with blending is not seen. SwiftKV/YOCO are architecture/distillation changes, not training-free chunk reuse.

Overall: partly covered, with MoE routing drift and the equal-budget layer-tiered comparison the clearest open pieces; dense per-layer profiling is partially done by 2603.20218, so E3 should cite and extend it (add token type/graph role, top-k capture) rather than claim novelty on its own. Caveat: many 2026 preprints were checked on abstract pages only; full-text layer analyses (RelaxKV, CacheClip, Kamera, KVBoost) should be read before final novelty claims.
